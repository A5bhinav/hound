# Make Hound's three minutes land like winners'

Past winners at Cal Hacks, TreeHacks, PennApps and HackMIT mostly won with things judges could watch happen: a sentence a judge can repeat, one visible "it actually happened" moment (a call answered, an alert firing, an incident staged at the venue), and real data stated as numbers and with the word "real" said out loud. The technical depth went into the Devpost write-up and the Q&A. Hound already has most of this. It has a real DOJ case, a blind scoreboard (8/8 intermediate wallets and 3/3 seized wallets), and a reveal that works. The gaps are in what judges actually see. The voice intake gets described but never heard. There is no Devpost video, even though Cal Hacks 13.0's Functionality criterion now requires one for sponsors judging asynchronously. The Devpost text and all four gallery images show a product that no longer exists. And the pitch is written for 2 minutes when 13.0 gives teams **3 minutes plus 2 minutes of questions** and adds a new **Intentionality** criterion. With about 18 hours left, here are the highest-value moves, in order. Rewrite PITCH.md around a 3:00 default that plays a real call and states who each design choice is for. Record a roughly 2:15 Devpost video tonight on the final UI. Rewrite DEVPOST.md and re-capture its gallery, using the exact prize names on Devpost. Then reconcile the Q&A numbers and rehearse table operations. None of this needs new features, and adding features is the classic way teams lose the demo.

## Winners show the thing happening, then prove it with real numbers

The grand-prize pattern at Cal Hacks since 2023 is a familiar interface that talks back. FaceTimeOS (12.0, 1st) let you FaceTime your Mac and have an agent do the task, speak in the call, and send a screenshot as proof. Duet (11.0, 1st) turned live EEG into music. Every top-three project from 11.0 and 12.0 had live audio or voice somewhere in the loop ([FaceTimeOS](https://devpost.com/software/facetime-macos-ai-agent), [Duet](https://devpost.com/software/duet-0tbkxe)). The FaceTimeOS team's own post-mortem is the most useful primary source. They had one person own the UI and demo, they **removed unreliable features from the demo path entirely** (keyboard-only agent, purpose-built tools in place of flaky clicks), and they kept a hardcoded fallback. They made the video in a Waymo 30 minutes before judging ([Dylan Lu blog](https://blog.dylanlu.com/cal-hacks-12/)).

Outside Cal Hacks, the openings fall into three types: the product responding within seconds, a shocking stat plus a one-line pitch, or an "X for Y" analogy. TreeHacks 2026's grand prize, Shepherd, opened with "This is Shepherd, a smart cane that guides the visually impaired" and then a voice command the cane obeyed, and only afterwards explained the problem ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nJ5YjK1-0c0)). Public-safety winners **staged the incident rather than describing it**. HawkWatch (TreeHacks 2025, 1st) had a teammate fake a faint on webcam and showed the alert email arriving ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h2G_Z_NtcXk)). Watchful.AI (PennApps XXV, Most Technically Complex) had a teammate "walk around with a knife" on a hand-built map of the venue ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ke7UuqB-MVk)).

The closest structural analogue to Hound is **Tribune** (TreeHacks 2026, Anthropic Human Flourishing). It took a phone agent, a real civic dataset and a cited output. Its 119-second video spends about 30 seconds on one real call ("Real phone call, real phone number"), shows the call ending and quotes being extracted automatically, and uses the word "real" five times. Its gallery captions are claims rather than decoration ("200 real Palo Alto policies", "35 residents interviewed, every quote linked to the policy clause it references") ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CNRvU4jK34c), [Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/tribune)).

At Cal Hacks itself, the closest analogue is **Expungo** (12.0 Greatest Social Impact plus Promise Public Impact). It combined a vulnerable user, document upload, rules-based eligibility, a voice agent that walks the user through it, and in-memory processing presented as a trust feature ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/clearpathai)). The Promise rubric that Expungo won spelled out what social-impact judges reward: underserved communities, "communication AI (voice, chat, etc.) to drive down barrier of entry", and pragmatism ([Cal Hacks 12.0](https://cal-hacks-12-0.devpost.com/)). **Guardian**, a 12.0 sponsor winner for scam prevention aimed at seniors, used Claude-written, senior-friendly explanations spoken aloud and a "dispute kit" as its output artifact ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/guardian-ai-powered-fraud-prevention-for-your-money)).

| Winner | What judges saw | Hound's equivalent today | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tribune (TreeHacks 26, Anthropic) | Real call audio → automatic cited output | Cartesia call → case card fills → trace → packet that "can only cite numbers in the trace" | Call is described at 0:15, never heard; "Play a sample call" is silent text |
| Expungo (Cal Hacks 12, Social Impact) | Voice agent guiding a vulnerable user; privacy stated | Disclosure-first agent, case code spelled twice, "Hound will never" list | Accessibility design not visible in any demo artifact |
| HawkWatch / Watchful (staged incident) | Teammate plays the victim; alert fires | Judge-as-victim with A.H.'s real receipt (demo kit) | Optional ~60 s insert; good fit for the extra minute and Q&A |
| Freak in the Sheets, SARTech (Most Technically Complex) | Hard part said in one sentence | "Reaches every wallet the DOJ seized from the victim's report alone, with freezes hidden" | Needs one plain sentence plus an architecture image |
| TraceFund, Hearti (honesty rewarded) | "Simulated honestly"; confidence scores | Real/replayed/simulated split, REPLAY labels, stage rules | Already strong; keep it |

Proof at these events was almost always demonstrative rather than statistical. No sampled winner showed a precision/recall benchmark ([other winners notes, Tribune/DriveWise](https://devpost.com/software/drivewise-g09s5y)). That makes Hound's **blind scoreboard against a real federal forfeiture case** unusual, and worth leading with, especially for the technical tracks. Technical-complexity winners can state their hard part in one sentence: "an LLVM backend that compiles to Google Sheets" ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/freak-in-the-sheets-7jl542)), or "a Monte Carlo search model of how lost people move" ([Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/sartech)). No crypto-forensics project has won a top prize at any of the ~350 winners scraped. That cuts both ways: Hound is novel, and judges have no reference point for it, so the one-sentence framing matters even more.

## Cal Hacks 13.0 changes the scoring math in three ways

First, the format is now **three minutes to pitch and two minutes of questions**, up from 2 + 2 in 10.0–12.0, and teams "must have a pre-recorded or live demonstration" ([Cal Hacks 13.0](https://cal-hacks-13-0.devpost.com/)). PITCH.md still treats 2:00 as the default and 3:00 as "additions" (PITCH.md:5-37), so the version Hound rehearses most is the wrong length. Second, the new **Intentionality** criterion asks for "an intuitive, engaging experience for their target users, guided by clear first principles. Every design choice should feel purposeful, with a clear sense of who it's for and why it works the way it does" ([Cal Hacks 13.0](https://cal-hacks-13-0.devpost.com/)). Hound has unusually principled design: phone first because victims can't read out a hash, warn-not-hold, never touching funds, a packet that can only cite traced numbers, and a calm Public Sans look chosen for the "register those readers already trust" (layout.tsx:5-9). The current script mentions none of these as choices. Third, Functionality now requires "a clear, meaningful video demo on devpost for sponsors evaluating asynchronously" ([Cal Hacks 13.0](https://cal-hacks-13-0.devpost.com/)). Hound has no video at all (DEMO.md:14 is unchecked, and no .mp4 or .mov exists in the repo). Microsoft's prize includes presenting "remotely" to its Frontier Co. FDE team, which suggests at least part of that judging happens off the floor ([Cal Hacks 13.0](https://cal-hacks-13-0.devpost.com/)). For those judges, **the video is the demo**.

The way judges score also shapes the pitch. MLH's recommended expo plan has each judge stack-rank a top three instead of giving absolute scores ([MLH Guide](https://guide.mlh.com/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan.md)). HackMIT's Gavel compares each project with the previous one, because entries "all start to blur together" and the average judge sees about 5% of projects ([Athalye](https://anishathalye.com/designing-a-better-judging-system/)). So the real goal is to be the most memorable of the handful of tables a judge saw. One judge who scored for a year says it plainly: rehearse the first 30 seconds, because "That is where the score is set". He also says "Pick the sentence you want a judge to repeat to another judge an hour later", and adds that "Admitting a limitation raised scores in my sheets" ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). Repeat winner John Kim's lesson for sponsor tracks is "The sponsors are the judges… They're in the room", so name each sponsor's tool on stage ([Push To Prod](https://getpushtoprod.substack.com/p/how-to-win-hackathons)).

The **prize names on Devpost differ from BRIEF.md**. Devpost lists "Most Technically **Advanced** Hack" and "Best People-First **Social Impact** Hack", where BRIEF says "Most Technically Complex" and "Best People-First". It also lists "[Anthropic] Claude for Public Benefit", "[Microsoft] Built for the Real World / Customer Impact Award", "[Cartesia] Best Use of Cartesia", "[Armadin] Best Security Hack" and "[Hyphenate] Autonomous Systems for Finance" ([Cal Hacks 13.0](https://cal-hacks-13-0.devpost.com/)). Tick the Devpost names. None of Hound's sponsor prizes publish criteria, so judges will fall back on the event rubric and their own company's lens. Where the same sponsors did publish criteria before, they rewarded depth of product use, real-world impact, and a demo that "holds up live". Anthropic weighted Demo at 30% in its own hackathon ([rules copy](https://huggingface.co/datasets/introvoyz041/postvisit/blob/main/data/docs/hackathon-rules.md), secondary). Its 12.0 prize asked for technical complexity, creative use and impact ([Cal Hacks 12.0](https://cal-hacks-12-0.devpost.com/)). BRIEF's claim that 25% of Cartesia's judging is accessibility and responsible use could not be verified publicly, but it fits Cartesia's past winners, such as Form Wizard Pro, which turned forms into voice "more natural and accessible" ([Cartesia blog](https://cartesia.ai/blog/hacktober)).

| 13.0 criterion | What wins it (evidence) | The Hound line or screen that answers it |
|---|---|---|
| Application | Names who it helps and why they'd use it | "A.H., a real victim; one report protects the next person" + exchange hold on /exchange |
| **Intentionality** (new) | Each design choice has a stated reason and audience | Phone because victims can't read a 64-character hash; warn-not-hold; never touches funds; packet cites only traced numbers |
| Functionality | Smooth live demo **and** a Devpost video | Offline replay path on localhost; ~2:15 video with the call audio |
| Creativity | Shows what is different from similar work | "Hound sees no DOJ labels and no freezes, and still lands on the seized wallets" (vs Chainalysis/TRM, who serve investigators, not victims) |
| Technical Complexity | Command of the architecture | One-screen architecture image; 353 tests; holdout guard; swap resolution and confidence per hop |

## Hound's hardest part already works; what judges see does not

The inventory in hound_current_demo.md shows a team whose engineering is ahead of its packaging. The emotional peak, **Reveal what Tether did**, is presenter-gated and lands well. TraceGraph unfolds one transfer every 900 ms and ends with "The engine never saw it. Tether froze 3 of these wallets 16 to 20 days after the victim's report" (TraceGraph.tsx:256-287, 388-395). The proof numbers are deterministic and offline-safe: the scoreboard comes from `backend/hound/proof/doj_ohnd.py`, and the backtest and weekend scan from committed JSON under `data/proof/`. That means the proof beat carries no network risk. A check of origin/main at 159097b also shows that two weaknesses flagged in the notes are already fixed. /proof renders big stat tiles again (8/8, 3/3, the per-wallet bands, 45 days, $91,218) after commit 33a0f3f, and the step bar on phones became one swipeable row in f086ac7. Don't spend time on either.

What remains is concentrated in three places. **The voice moment is invisible.** PITCH.md:10-11 describes the call in 15 seconds. "Play a sample call" plays five silent text frames (LiveIntake.tsx:102-118). PITCH's 3-minute version calls for a "recorded intake clip" that does not exist anywhere in the repo, even though docs/CARTESIA_AGENT.md:18-23 lists recording it as a playground step. Every voice winner in the evidence (Tribune, Expungo, FaceTimeOS, SpeakEasy) made the voice audible, so this is the biggest gap for Cartesia, People-First and Claude for Public Benefit. **The Devpost is stale.** DEVPOST.md:24 still names Cytoscape (the graph is hand-drawn SVG ribbons). DEVPOST.md:33 names the "Replay intake" button (it is now **Play a sample call**). DEVPOST.md:39 lists cytoscape under Built with. DEVPOST.md:42 lists "Cartesia (if voice ships)" and "Apify (if the scraper ships)", and no Apify code exists. The page has no backtest number, no weekend-scan number, no phone number, no public URL and no try-it instructions. All four gallery PNGs in docs/devpost/ predate the redesigns. 1-trace.png shows the old Cytoscape box graph and old navigation, and 4-proof.png shows "Not computed yet" for the weekend scan, which undercuts the $91K headline. **The Q&A numbers disagree with each other.** QA_PREP.md:42 gives Operation Level Up figures that BRIEF §2 says not to use. QA_PREP.md:4 says "200 of the 657" where backtest.json measured n = 192. QA_PREP.md:25 describes a live case (96,028 USDT to Coinbase) that doesn't match PITCH.md:34 (11,082 USDT, 85% to Binance). A judge who hears two versions of a number stops trusting all of them.

The main structural risk is unchanged. The public path runs Cartesia → ngrok → laptop → Vercel. It is the least tested (e2e.py never places a call) and has the most hops, and no record of a successful end-to-end live call exists in the repo or git log (hound_current_demo.md §5). That supports the FaceTimeOS rule: keep the flaky path out of the default demo. **The default should play a recorded real call, and a live call should be an offer for Cartesia judges or anyone who asks.** CARTESIA_AGENT.md:3 already planned it that way. The home-page copy and the voice-agent prompt have already been fixed on main, and the leftover "Gets a text link" on /how (how/page.tsx:35) and DEMO.md:50 are being fixed now, so they are not repeated below.

### Rank 1: a 3:00 script built on the winners' skeleton

Judge advice converges on a fixed sequence. State what it is in the first sentence. Give one concrete person and one number. Get the product working on screen fast. Show the happy path ending on the hardest, cleverest thing. Mention the technology only after the judge cares. Close with a sentence meant to be repeated ([Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/investors.html), [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/), [Eden](https://shkspr.mobi/blog/2019/11/how-to-present-at-a-hack-day/)). PG's limit of "four or five numbers" is a real constraint for Hound, because PITCH.md currently says more than a dozen. The extra minute should go to the two beats the notes identify as weak: the call (0:15, described rather than shown) and the packet and proof (10 s each). It should not go to more numbers. Below is a version that reuses PITCH.md's verified lines.

| Time | Screen | Beat (reuse PITCH.md wording; new lines in quotes) |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:12 | `/how` H1 | "Hound is a 911 call for crypto scam victims: it follows stolen crypto to where it can still be frozen." Then the FBI IC3 $4.4B line |
| 0:12–0:40 | `/intake`, call clip playing | "This is a real call to our real number." ~20 s of audio: disclosure, first name, code spelled twice. Intentionality line: "Phone first, because the people this happens to can't read a 64-character hash, and the agent never asks for money or keys." |
| 0:40–1:30 | `/cases/[id]` TraceGraph | US v. 4.34M Tether, A.H.; "Hound sees no DOJ labels and no freezes." Let hops unfold (use **Skip to the end** if behind), then **Reveal what Tether did** |
| 1:30–1:55 | `/exchange` | **Try: A wallet from the DOJ case** → **Withdrawal held**; hot wallet → **Withdrawal sent**. "We warn, not seize: the exchange's fraud team decides, with the evidence." |
| 1:55–2:20 | `/cases/[id]/packet` | Detective/victim toggle. "Claude writes it, but it can only cite numbers that are in the trace." |
| 2:20–2:45 | `/proof` stat tiles | 8/8 and 3/3; two seized wallets within ~1%, the third within 11; "Every wallet here is real." |
| 2:45–3:00 | `/proof` | Sponsor credits in one breath (Cartesia voice, Claude vision + writer), then "Hound. The 911 call for crypto scam victims." |

That keeps the spoken numbers to five: $4.4B, 8/8, 3/3, 16 to 20 days, and ~1%/11%. The backtest's 45 days and the $91,218 weekend scan go into the Q&A and the Devpost. The judge-as-victim insert (docs/demo-kit/README.md: A.H.'s receipt at /demo/receipt.png, read on chain in ~2 s) should be offered at the start of Q&A ("Want to be the victim?"). That puts it where HawkWatch and Watchful put their staged incidents, and judge advice says hands-on time is remembered ([Devpost judges](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)). Run the script out loud three times against a timer, and write the actual times into PITCH.md. The notes found no rehearsal timing recorded anywhere.

## Conclusion

Hound's Devpost and pitch read as if technical rigor were the point, and for one track (Most Technically Advanced) it is. But winners for every other prize Hound is entering got there by letting judges witness something: a voice answering, an alert firing, a real person served. Hound has the rare combination of a witnessable moment (the call, the reveal) and statistical proof that most winners never had (a blind match against a federal forfeiture). Right now it shows the proof and hides the moment. The extra minute in 13.0 and the new Intentionality criterion both reward the reverse: let judges hear the call, and say why each design choice exists, and they will believe the numbers that follow.

There are real unknowns. No sponsor on Hound's list has published criteria for 13.0. Whether 13.0 has a stage-finalist round is unpublished. No Cal Hacks winner has ever done blockchain forensics, so there is no precedent for how judges react to one. Whether a teammate already has a video or a newer Devpost draft outside the repo (DEVPOST.md says Aaryan owns final edits) is also unknown, so check before duplicating work. These unknowns argue for the plan above rather than against it. A video that stands alone, a page mapped to every criterion, and a script that uses all three minutes perform well whichever way the open questions turn out.
